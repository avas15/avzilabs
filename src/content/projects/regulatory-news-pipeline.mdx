Spectrum moves through publications: a consultation opens, an award is announced, a band plan is
amended, an assignment changes hands. It is all public, and it is scattered across dozens of
regulator sites in several languages, on no common schedule and in no common format.

The old approach was checking manually and paying a third party for the rest. This replaced
both.

## The pipeline

A scheduled daily sweep across regulator sites and industry sources. Each source has its own
adapter, because they genuinely are all different: some publish clean RSS, some have a search
page that must be walked, and some post PDFs to a directory listing with no index at all.

Fetched documents are normalised, deduplicated, classified by band and country, and summarised.
Everything lands in the same structured database the rest of the spectrum tooling reads from, so
a change in a regulator's publication becomes a change in the internal record rather than an
email somebody has to action.

## The parts that were actually hard

**Deduplication.** The same award gets covered by the regulator, two trade publications and an
operator press release, all with different wording. Exact matching catches almost none of it.
Near-duplicate detection on embeddings plus a date and entity check gets most of it, and the
threshold is a genuine tradeoff: too tight and the feed is repetitive, too loose and a real
follow-up development gets swallowed as a duplicate of the original story.

**Change detection.** A regulator quietly amending a page is more interesting than a new
publication, and much harder to notice. Content hashing per source, with the noisy furniture
stripped out first, otherwise a rotating banner marks the whole page as changed every single
day.

**Failing loudly.** A scraper that silently returns nothing is worse than one that crashes,
because the feed just looks quiet and everyone assumes nothing happened. Each source has an
expected volume range and alerts when it falls outside, so a site redesign surfaces as an alert
rather than as a gap somebody notices weeks later.

**Classification.** Band and country extraction is mostly deterministic parsing with an LLM
fallback for the awkward cases, not the other way round. Regex is cheaper, faster and more
predictable for the ninety percent that follows a pattern; the model handles the tail.

## What it changed

It removed a recurring third-party data cost, and more usefully it changed the latency. A
consultation opening is known the day it opens rather than whenever somebody happened to check,
and the database behind the benchmarking and valuation work is current by default rather than as
a result of somebody remembering to update it.
